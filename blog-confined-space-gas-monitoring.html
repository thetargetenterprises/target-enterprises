Confined Space Entry: 4-Gas Monitoring & Standby Attendant SOP

✍️ Target Enterprises EHS Engineering ⏱️ 6 Min Read 📅 September 2026 📍 Confined Space Safety Cell

Entering storage tanks, boiler drums, electrostatic precipitator (ESP) hoppers, and underground culverts carries lethal unseen dangers: toxic gas accumulation, flammable vapors, and rapid oxygen depletion. Confined space accidents frequently result in multiple fatalities when untrained rescuers rush in unprotected.

1. The 4-Gas Testing Thresholds

Prior to breaking the plane of any manhole, an authorized Gas Tester must sample atmospheric levels at the top, middle, and bottom using an aspirated, calibrated 4-gas detector:

✔
Oxygen (O₂): Safe range strictly between 19.5% and 23.5%. Levels below 19.5% require immediate air-supplied breathing apparatus (SCBA).
✔
Flammable Gases (LEL): Must read 0% LEL for hot work, and strictly under 5% LEL for inspection.
✔
Carbon Monoxide (CO): Toxic threshold must not exceed 25 ppm (8-hour TWA).
✔
Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S): Extremely lethal sewer/hydrocarbon gas; must not exceed 5 ppm.

2. The Crucial Role of the Standby Sentry

A Standby Safety Attendant must remain permanently stationed outside the manhole entrance throughout the entry operation. The sentry maintains the log of entrants, ensures forced ventilation fans (air eductors) remain active, and coordinates emergency sirens—never entering the vessel under any circumstances.

Mandatory Retrieval Gear: Every entrant must wear a full-body rescue harness connected via retrieval line to a heavy-duty tripod and recovery winch positioned over the entry manhole.
